How to actually keep a New Year's resolution

Updated October 2026 · 6 minute read

The short answer: shrink the resolution into one small daily action, attach it to something you already do, keep a visible reminder of it on your phone, and treat a missed day as a blip rather than a failure. A streak you can see, and a reason to protect it, does the rest.

Why resolutions fade

January resolutions tend to be big ("get fit", "read more", "learn Spanish") and vague about what to do on a Tuesday night when you're tired. Motivation is high on January 1st and then falls away, so anything that depends on motivation alone fades with it. Habits are different: they run on cues and repetition, not willpower.

Habits also take longer to form than the "21 days" rule suggests. In a well-known study at University College London, it took people a median of 66 days for a new daily behaviour to feel automatic, with a very wide range from person to person (Lally et al., 2010). Plan for a couple of months, not a few weeks.

1. Make it small enough to do on a bad day

Turn the resolution into one action so small you can't talk yourself out of it. "Read more" becomes "read 10 pages". "Get fit" becomes "a 10 minute walk". You can always do more, but the habit is the minimum. A tiny habit you keep beats an ambitious one you drop.

2. Give it a cue

Decide exactly when and where it happens: "after I pour my morning coffee, I'll stretch for five minutes." Psychologists call this an implementation intention, and it works because the decision is already made before the moment arrives. Attaching the new habit to one you already have (habit stacking) is the easiest way to find a cue.

3. Keep it in sight

Out of sight really is out of mind. Put the reminder somewhere you look dozens of times a day: your phone's Home Screen or Lock Screen. A widget that shows whether today's habit is done is a cue you can't avoid. That's why we built Habit Pups widgets for both.

4. Track it, and make the streak worth protecting

A streak turns "should I bother today?" into "I don't want to break it." It works best when the streak means something. In Habit Pups each habit is a puppy: check it off and it celebrates; keep going and it learns tricks at 3, 7, 10 and 14 days, then earns toys, sunglasses, a cape and eventually a crown.

5. Never miss twice

You will miss a day. That's normal, and it isn't where resolutions die. They die when one missed day becomes "I've blown it". The rule that helps most is simple: never miss twice in a row. In Habit Pups a missed day makes your pup look a little sad, and one check-off the next day perks it right back up. Nothing is lost, and the tricks it learned stay learned.

6. Pick a schedule that fits your life

Not every habit needs to be daily. Running on weekdays, journaling three times a week and stretching every day are all fine, as long as the schedule is honest. Days a habit doesn't ask for shouldn't count against you.

A resolution plan you can copy

  1. Write the resolution as one small action with a cue: "After dinner, I'll practise Spanish for 10 minutes."
  2. Choose its schedule: every day, certain days, or a few times a week.
  3. Set one reminder for just before the cue.
  4. Put it on your Lock Screen or Home Screen.
  5. Check it off the moment it's done.
  6. If you miss a day, do the smallest possible version tomorrow.
